BEAm Bus Engagement AMplified

The BEAm (Bus Engagement AMplified) environment is a set of web browser-based tools that we have been developing since fiscal year 2019 with the aim of turning route bus operation information into information landscapes. By an information landscape, we mean a "landscape" generated by visually and semantically superimposing public transportation operation information onto the shape of a town and representing it three-dimensionally, so that residents can perceive, in multiple layers and in connection with geographic space, the elements that are considered important or are favored in the community. The users of the technologies developed in this research are not limited to passengers and operators; they include the general public in the region, visitors and newcomers to the region, people outside the region who happen to become interested in it, and researchers in local history and the social sciences.

Macro Landscaping

Macro landscaping refers to treating a large area, such as what can be captured in an aerial photograph or a whole town viewed from high ground, as a single landscape. Figure 1 shows a pseudo-3D display linked with a map, created using route-specific timetables, route-specific sequences of stops, and the locations (latitude and longitude) of bus stops (bus stop poles). With the map as the base, a time axis extends from the coordinates of each bus stop (the top is 0:00 and the bottom is 24:00). For each route, the departure times at each bus stop are plotted and connected with lines. The figure shows all routes in part of the Hakodate Bus service area and the entire extent of one route. This makes it possible to visually confirm, for example, that when the bus of interest is at this point, a bus on another route is running around there.

Micro Landscaping

Micro landscaping refers to localized landscaping within the range one can see when standing at a certain point at a certain time. We have begun structuring, as a graph, the sequences of previous and next bus stops for every route that passes through each bus stop. Figure 2 shows, for the Hakodate Bus stop "Kashiwagi-cho," the routes grouped by identical sequences of preceding and following stops.

A Prototype Tool that Superimposes Bus Service Frequency on a Map as a Visual Representation

Figure 3 shows bus service frequency for a specified section in a pseudo-3D display. In the tool, the map can be tilted and rotated. The positions of the bus stop poles (stops) included in the routes in the GTFS data are identified and plotted on the map as the base. The segments between stops are connected and represented as surfaces whose heights correspond to service frequency.
